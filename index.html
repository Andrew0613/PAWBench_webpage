PAWBench

How Far Are We from Probabilistically Aligned World Modeling?

Benchmark & protocol: formalizes probabilistic alignment with 50 scenarios across eight physical mechanism groups; PAWEval turns repeated rollouts into outcome distributions.
Model diagnosis: benchmarks eleven current video generators and shows that plausible rollouts can still miss valid futures or assign them the wrong probability mass; the gap persists beyond finite sampling and automated outcome readout.
Distribution shaping: probes language guidance, initial-noise sampling, and fine-tuning to distinguish inference-time steering and exploration from changes to the learned predictive distribution.

How far are current video generators from probabilistically aligned world modeling?

Recent video generation models are increasingly framed as world models. Many physical processes can unfold in more than one valid way, so a world model should reproduce not only a plausible trajectory, but also the distribution of possible behaviors under the same initial observation and action.

Existing evaluations largely assess individual-video plausibility and do not test whether repeated generations recover the correct distribution. PAWBench formalizes this distribution-level requirement as probabilistic alignment.

PAWBench: A Benchmark for Possible Futures

PAWBench evaluates a video generation model through repeated rollouts conditioned on the same initial observation and action. Each benchmark item provides a source image, an action prompt, a finite set of valid terminal outcomes, and, when available, a reference distribution over those outcomes.

PAWEval: Outcome-Level Evaluation for Repeated Rollouts

PAWEval maps generated rollouts to terminal physical outcomes. Gemini 3.5 Flash applies a frozen, scene-specific rubric and returns either an in-schema outcome label or an outcome-readout failure. Readable, in-schema outcome labels are normalized into the conditional empirical distribution used by PAW-Calibration and PAW-Coverage; model-scene pairs become scoreable only after passing the outcome-readout gate.

State-of-the-Art Model Performance on PAWBench

The main evaluation samples K=50 independent rollouts per scenario and system. A scene passes the outcome-readout gate when at least 20 of 50 rollouts yield readable, in-schema outcomes. Scene Pass Rate (SPR) is the percentage of all 25 scenes in the corresponding track that pass this gate.

Main PAWBench results. PAW-Calibration reports conditional TVD ×100 (lower is better); a 70/30 model distribution against a 50/50 reference scores 20, while an exact match scores 0. PAW-Coverage reports conditional valid-support recovery percentage (higher is better). Avg. is computed over passing scenes; SPR is the percentage of all 25 scenes that pass. N/A means no scene in that mechanism group passes the gate.

Distribution-Shaping Interventions and Controlled Diagnostics

PAWBench tests whether language conditioning, initial-noise sampling, or model training can reshape predictive distributions. Language can request individual futures, coupled noise can broaden finite-budget exploration, and fine-tuning can shift outcome frequencies; none reliably recovers the scene-conditioned distribution across different physical states.

Language steering

Prompt engineering and oracle target prompts separate future selection from video realization. Even explicit targets are realized only 37.6-58.1% of the time.

Conclusion

PAWBench shows that plausible, diverse, or controllable rollouts do not by themselves establish probabilistically aligned world modeling. Across eleven current video generators, no model consistently matches the reference probabilities while recovering the range of valid futures.
